# Product

## Register

product

Reader-facing surfaces (article pages, book chapters, publisher profiles, the homepage) take the **brand** register per-task: there, the reading experience IS the product. Authoring, moderation, analytics, and settings surfaces stay product. This dual register is deliberate — see Design Principles.

## Platform

web

## Users

A community of publishers writing serious mathematical and scientific content — today anchored by the founding author using it as a personal textbook of everything, with the multi-publisher and organization machinery built to host others alongside. Their readers are students and curious minds consuming rigorous long-form content: articles, curriculum books, citations, and discussion. Phase 2 broadens the audience to people who purely love writing and blogging, served by a general-purpose editor with Notion-competing UX — added without eroding what phase 1 built.

## Product Purpose

Bridge what today lives in three separate worlds: arXiv-grade rigor, blog-easy publishing, and textbook-grade coherence — everything in one place, beautiful enough that a user can read and work for hours without tiring. Success is threefold and cumulative: a coherent, interlinked, beautifully typeset personal corpus the author still enjoys years on; readers who genuinely read, comment, and return; and the multi-publisher machinery hosting more than the founder's own work.

## Positioning

The one place where arXiv rigor, blogging ease, and textbook coherence meet — and stay beautiful for hours at a time.

## Brand Personality

Scholarly, calm, timeless. A quiet library: authority without stiffness, nothing trendy, comfortable for long sessions. The voice is confident and precise, never promotional.

## Anti-references

- **Web-1.0 academic**: arXiv / LaTeX-homepage rawness — dense, unstyled pages hostile to sustained reading. Rigor must never look raw.
- **Cluttered wiki**: Wikipedia-style chrome overload — boxes, sidebars, and link thickets fighting the prose for attention.

## Design Principles

1. **Built for the long session.** Every choice — typography, contrast, spacing, motion restraint — is judged by whether a reader or writer can stay for hours without fatigue.
2. **Dual register, one soul.** Reading surfaces get editorial gravitas (design IS the product); tools disappear into the task (design SERVES the product). Both share the same calm, scholarly voice.
3. **Rigor without rawness.** Math, citations, versioning, and structure are first-class citizens, but never at the cost of beauty — the explicit refusal of the arXiv aesthetic.
4. **One corpus, many forms.** The same writing flows between wiki article, book chapter, PDF, and EPUB without losing coherence; design must honor that continuity.
5. **Grow without regression.** Phase 2's general writing tools must never erode phase 1's textbook-grade publishing; new audiences are added, existing ones never traded away.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA: contrast ≥4.5:1 for body text, full keyboard navigability with visible focus, and reduced-motion support throughout. This is treated as inseparable from the long-session comfort goal, not a compliance checkbox.
